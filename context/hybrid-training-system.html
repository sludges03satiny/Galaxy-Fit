01

HIGH-LEVEL PHILOSOPHY

The system is built to last years, not weeks. Every structural decision exists to prevent the two failure modes you've shown: burning out when things get hard and fading when structure disappears. The antidote isn't more discipline — it's better architecture.

Strength = Foundation

Heavy compounds come first. They drive bone density, hormonal output, and joint health — the physical infrastructure that makes everything else work. No junk volume.

Axial loading Bone density Neural efficiency

Skill = Practice, Not Training

Handstand, levers, L-sit — these are done fresh, submaximal, and frequent. They have their own progression gates and are never placed after fatiguing work.

Fresh CNS Submaximal High frequency

VO₂max = Multiplier

Cardiovascular capacity raises the ceiling on everything else. It's periodized across blocks — not the same intervals forever — to prevent adaptation plateau and burnout.

Periodized Rotating formats Zone 2 base

Mobility = Progressive Overload

Flexibility is trained like strength — with increasing ROM, then load, then active control. Static holds forever don't produce structural change.

Loaded stretching ROM overload Active flexibility

Sustainability = Architecture

The system doesn't collapse when you miss days. Rolling A/B/C, autoregulation rules, and defined minimum doses make it resilient by design, not by willpower.

Autoregulation Min effective dose No punishment

Macrocycles = Longevity

Every 12 weeks is a block. Blocks repeat with elevated baselines forever. This is how you train for years without plateau or boredom.

Acc → Int → Real Baseline elevation Annual planning

Core principle: You are building a system that produces 10 good years, not 10 great weeks. Every decision optimizes for consistency over intensity, and skill development over performance PR-chasing.

02

WEEKLY TRAINING TEMPLATE

You train in a rolling A → B → C sequence. Days have no fixed weekday assignment. Miss a day — continue where you left off. Aim for 3–5 sessions per week. If spontaneous activity happens, insert it as a Zone 2 or mobility day and continue the sequence.

Spontaneous activity rule: Any unplanned activity of 45+ min (hike, sport, yoga, cycling) counts as a conditioning or mobility day. Log it, continue the A/B/C sequence next session. No guilt. No "makeup" sessions.

Session Tiers

MINIMUM — 30 MIN

  • 5 min warm-up (dynamic only)
  • 5 min skill practice
  • 1 main compound lift — 3 sets
  • 1 accessory — 2 sets

This session always counts. Never skip because you only have 30 min.

STANDARD — 45 MIN

  • 8 min warm-up + mobility flow
  • 8 min skill practice
  • 20 min strength (2 lifts)
  • 8 min conditioning or accessory

OPTIMAL — 60 MIN

  • 10 min warm-up
  • 10 min skill
  • 30 min strength (3 lifts)
  • 10 min accessory or intervals

The A / B / C Structure

Day Focus Main Lifts Skill Conditioning
A Lower Body Squat, RDL, Split Squat Handstand Optional short intervals
B Upper Push Bench/OHP, Weighted Dip L-sit, Back Lever (skill only) HIIT finisher (8–12 min)
C Upper Pull Weighted Pull-up, Row, Deadlift Front Lever tuck holds — low volume, fresh None (save CNS)
Z Zone 2 / Outdoor — Optional skill play 60–90 min bike/hike

Front lever note: Front lever practice on C day is placed at the START, before any pulling volume. Keep it to 4–5 sets of holds only. Once you begin heavy pulling, lever work is done for the session.

Recommended Weekly Rhythm (Target)

A → B → rest/Z → C → rest → A → B (then continue). In practice: just do the next letter when you train. The sequence self-corrects.

03

STRENGTH PROGRAM

Strength is the foundation of bone density, hormonal health, and calisthenics performance. We use double progression with clear entry points for your detrained state, and a macrocycle that evolves every block.

Progression Model: Double Progression

Rule: Every lift has a rep range (e.g. 4×5–8). When all 4 sets hit the top of the range (8 reps) at RPE ≤ 8 → increase load next session (2.5–5kg lower body, 1.25–2.5kg upper). When you can't hit the bottom of the range (5 reps) → reduce load 10% and rebuild.

Phase-by-Phase Lifting Protocol

Phase Weeks Sets × Reps RPE Load Start Priority
Phase 1 — Rebuild 1–4 3–4 × 5–8 6–7 60–65% estimated 1RM Motor pattern restoration, tendon tolerance
Phase 2 — Accumulate 6–9 4 × 4–6 7–8 75–80% 1RM Volume and strength building
Phase 3 — Intensify 11–12 4–5 × 3–5 8–9 82–88% 1RM Peak strength, bone loading
Deload 5, 10 3 × 5–8 5–6 50–60% 1RM Recovery, movement practice

Lift Selection Per Day

DAY A — LOWER

  • Primary: Back Squat (or Goblet → Front Squat progression)
  • Hinge: Romanian Deadlift
  • Accessory: Bulgarian Split Squat or Step-up
  • Core: Ab wheel or hanging knee raise

DAY B — PUSH

  • Primary: Barbell Bench Press
  • Secondary: Overhead Press
  • Accessory: Weighted Dips
  • Core: L-sit holds

DAY C — PULL

  • Primary: Weighted Pull-up
  • Secondary: Barbell Row or Pendlay Row
  • Hinge: Conventional Deadlift (alternates blocks with RDL)
  • Accessory: Face pull or band pull-apart

Deadlift rotation: In odd blocks, deadlift is on C day as listed. In even blocks, move it to A day as the primary hinge and rotate RDL to C as accessory. This prevents adaptation and manages fatigue accumulation.

12-Month Strength Benchmarks

1.8×BW Back Squat (Month 6)
2.2×BW Deadlift (Month 12)
+50%BW Weighted Pull-up
1×BW Overhead Press
+70%BW Weighted Dip

Bone Density Note

Bone density responds to axial loading under high tension over time. Squats and deadlifts are the primary drivers. You need consistent progressive loading across months and years — deloads are critical because bone remodels during recovery, not during the lift. Consistency > heroic single sessions.

04

CALISTHENICS SKILL ROADMAP

Each skill has a progression ladder with unlock gates. You must hit the gate criteria in 3 consecutive sessions before advancing. If stalled for 2+ weeks, follow the stall protocol. Skills are practiced fresh, before strength work.

Universal skill prescription: 3–5 sets per session. Stop 1–2 reps or 3–5 seconds before failure. Never train skills to exhaustion. Frequency 3–5×/week produces faster progress than 1–2×/week at higher intensity.

VO₂MAX & CONDITIONING

Conditioning is periodized across blocks — you do not run the same intervals forever. Each 12-week block has a conditioning emphasis that rotates to prevent adaptation and burnout.

Heart Rate Zones (Apple Watch)

Zone % Max HR BPM (Est. Max ~197) Feel Purpose
Zone 1 50–60% 99–118 Easy walk Active recovery
Zone 2 60–70% 118–138 Can hold conversation Aerobic base, fat metabolism, VO₂max base
Zone 3 70–80% 138–158 Harder to talk Aerobic threshold, avoid as default
Zone 4 80–90% 158–177 Hard, short sentences Lactate threshold, tempo work
Zone 5 90–100% 177–197 Max effort VO₂max intervals

Conditioning Per Block

BLOCK 1 — AEROBIC BASE

2× Zone 2 (30–45 min) + 1× short intervals

Intervals: 6–8 × 1 min Zone 4 / 2 min rest

Mountain bike counts as Zone 2 if HR stays in range.

Weeks 1–4

BLOCK 2 — VO₂MAX EMPHASIS

1× Zone 2 (45–60 min) + 2× VO₂max intervals

Intervals: 4–5 × 4 min Zone 5 / 3 min active rest

Or: 10 × 1 min max / 1 min rest

Weeks 6–9

BLOCK 3 — THRESHOLD

1× Zone 2 + 1× tempo + 1× HIIT

Tempo: 20–25 min continuous Zone 4
HIIT: Hill sprints 8–10 × 20 sec all-out / 40 sec rest

Weeks 11–12

Mountain Bike Integration

Mountain biking counts as Zone 2 if average HR stays 118–138. For hilly rides where HR spikes, count it as a mixed session and treat it as a conditioning day — skip the day's HIIT. Use Apple Watch to verify average HR post-ride.

Conditioning KPIs

+8% Apple Watch VO₂max (12 weeks)
−6 BPM Resting HR (12 weeks)
+2 reps 4×4 intervals completed (Block 2)

MOBILITY & FLEXIBILITY SYSTEM

Mobility is trained with progressive overload — the same logic as lifting. You increase ROM, then add load, then develop active control. Passive stretching alone produces temporary change. This system produces structural change.

Three-Phase Mobility Progression

Phase Method Example Duration
Phase 1 Passive ROM + end-range holds Pike stretch, couch stretch (60 sec holds) Weeks 1–4
Phase 2 Loaded stretching Jefferson curl with weight, weighted pike Weeks 5–9
Phase 3 Active flexibility Active leg raises, shrimp squat, active pancake Weeks 10+

Daily Micro-Routine (5–10 min, non-negotiable)

Movement Sets × Duration Progression
Jefferson Curl 2 × 8 reps (slow) Phase 1: bodyweight → Phase 2: +5–10kg → Phase 3: hold at end ROM 5 sec
Couch Stretch 1 × 60 sec/side Phase 1: foot on wall → Phase 2: add hip thrust active → Phase 3: rear foot elevated split squat
Dead Hang 2–3 × 30–60 sec Phase 1: passive → Phase 2: add active shoulder retraction → Phase 3: skin the cat
Deep Squat Hold 1–2 × 60 sec Phase 1: assisted → Phase 2: unassisted → Phase 3: heels elevated removed

Weekly Mobility Session (30 min, 1×/week)

Do this on a Z day, after a hike/bike, or as a standalone. Combine with yoga if you go spontaneously — that yoga session counts and replaces this.

  • Loaded pancake stretch (3 × 60 sec with gentle forward lean)
  • Pigeon pose with hip circles (2 min/side)
  • Thoracic rotation on foam roller (2 × 10/side)
  • Wall shoulder flexion (2 × 60 sec)
  • Straddle Jefferson curl (light, 3 × 8)
  • PNF hamstring stretch (3 × 6 sec contract / 20 sec relax/side)

TRACKING & METRICS SYSTEM

Track the minimum that tells you the most. One Google Sheet. Weekly check-in takes 5 minutes. Monthly benchmark day takes 20 minutes and gives you everything you need to make decisions.

What to Track vs. Ignore

✓ TRACK THESE

  • Lift: weight × reps per session (per main lift)
  • Skill: hold time in seconds + progression level (1–5)
  • VO₂max estimate (Apple Watch — check monthly)
  • Resting HR (Apple Watch — check weekly)
  • Flexibility: toe touch distance (cm from floor)
  • Sessions completed per week (count, not type)

✗ IGNORE THESE

  • Calories burned (irrelevant to your goals)
  • Daily bodyweight fluctuations
  • Apple "Move/Exercise minutes"
  • HRV (useful but adds complexity — skip for now)
  • Individual session RPE scores (too granular)

SUSTAINABILITY SYSTEM

This is the section that determines whether the system lasts 12 weeks or 12 years. Read it as carefully as the strength programming.

Decision Rules by Situation

Only have 30 min
Skill 5 min → Main lift 3×5 → Out. Session complete. Never skip because time is short.
Low motivation
Start. Commit to 10 minutes only. Permission to stop after. Most sessions proceed normally once started.
After 1 week off
Resume the sequence. Reduce loads 10–15%. No punishment sessions. No "catch-up." Continue as if week 1 of a new mini-block.
After 2–3 weeks off
Treat as deload week. 60% loads, all movements. Then rebuild from Phase 1 RPE targets for 2 weeks before normal progression.
Spontaneous hike / sport / yoga
Count it. Log it as Z day. Continue A/B/C sequence next training session. Never a "lost day."
Feel great, want more
Add one top set at RPE 8–9 on the main lift. Do not add extra sessions. Recovery is where gains are made.

Autoregulation: Sleep & Stress

Condition Adjustment
Sleep ≥7 hrs, stress ≤5/10 Green light. Train as programmed. Optional: add top set if feeling strong.
Sleep 6–7 hrs OR stress 6–7/10 Yellow light. Train as programmed, drop RPE target by 1. Remove HIIT finisher. Keep skill work.
Sleep <6 hrs OR stress ≥8/10 Red light. Skill + 1 main lift only. No conditioning. OR replace with 30 min Zone 1 walk + daily mobility.
Sick (any symptoms) Full rest. Daily mobility only. Return to training when symptom-free for 48 hrs.

Anti-burnout structure: Deload weeks are mandatory, not optional. Intensity drops on week 5 and 10 regardless of how you feel. The purpose of a deload is not because you're tired — it's to prevent you from needing to be tired to take one.

Burnout Prevention Rules

  • Never do HIIT two days in a row
  • Never do skill work post-strength failure sets
  • 3 high-intensity sessions per week is the absolute maximum
  • If resting HR rises 5+ BPM above your baseline for 3+ days: take 2 full rest days, reduce next week's load by 20%
  • Deload weeks are not optional — they are structural
09

MACRO CYCLE: INDEFINITELY SCALABLE

This is the piece that makes the system last years, not weeks. Every 12-week block follows the same structure but begins from an elevated baseline. Blocks accumulate. You never "start over" — you rebuild from a higher floor.

The Repeating Block Structure

WEEKS 1–4
ACCUMULATION

Higher volume, moderate intensity. Phase 1 programming (3–4×5–8 @ RPE 6–7). Conditioning: aerobic base emphasis. Skill: establish or rebuild current progression level.

WEEK 5
DELOAD

30–40% volume reduction. Keep movement quality. Benchmark day. Assess: what level did each skill reach? What did strength add? Plan next block's starting loads.

WEEKS 6–9
INTENSIFICATION

Lower volume, higher intensity. Phase 2 programming (4×4–6 @ RPE 7–8). Conditioning: VO₂max interval emphasis. Skill: advance one level if gates were met.

WEEK 10
DELOAD

Same as week 5. Second benchmark day of the block.

WEEKS 11–12
REALIZATION

Peak performance expression. Phase 3 programming (4–5×3–5 @ RPE 8–9). Conditioning: mixed HIIT/threshold. Skill: consolidate level gains. Test PRs where appropriate.

How blocks evolve: After each 12-week block, your starting loads for the next block are your Phase 3 working weights minus 10%. Your skill progression picks up where it left off. Conditioning format rotates (Block 1 aerobic → Block 2 VO₂max → Block 3 threshold → repeat). This is how you train for years without plateau.
